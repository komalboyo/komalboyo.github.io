An existential (and slightly sophomoric) attempt at a dissertation of

Stairway to Heaven

by Led Zeppelin

The horrors of having to choose, and the perils of over-intellectualising things.

June 2026 · Komal Mathur

This is not so much a dissertation of the lyrics themselves as a deeply personal and opinionated piece on where I find myself currently, with stray references to my enduring obsession with the global phenomenon by Led Zeppelin, Stairway to Heaven.

Just to preface, whether Robert Plant or Jimmy Page ever meant any of the nimiety of meanings fans and critics have found while immersed in the strong vocals and imagery of the song is beside the point. Take David Bowie’s word for it: “All art is unstable. Its meaning is not necessarily that implied by the author. There is no authoritative voice. There are only multiple readings.”

I personally think they just happened to string together the words in a way that, coupled with their ungodly instrumentation and vocals, spoke to people on so many levels. And we have no way to know what they were on, given it was the 70s rock scene.

Yes, there are two paths you can go by but in the long run, there’s still time to change the road you’re on. Your head is humming and it won’t go, in case you don’t know. The piper’s calling you to join him.

The sheer horror of being accountable to your future self with a decision made by your current self, who has limited ways to make an informed choice. The fear of regretting whatever you choose. Or maybe making the best of it. Is the key to just gain an optimistic attitude, or should you perhaps look back and learn? Which side should the pendulum swing?

I am currently at a crossroads, which forces me to introspect in a way I have perhaps avoided in the past. It necessitates that I ‘know what I am’. What my values are, what traits I possess innately, what drives me, and the hardest of them all perhaps — what I like doing. The more I think, the less I am. Such is the dilemma of the perpetual over-thinker. If you think long and hard about something, eventually you’d be able to find ways to counter every thought or feeling you’ve ever had (with the exception of a few — I call those my moral compass). In such a situation, how am I to ever recognise my gut feeling, or know what I like? All logic reaches an end, after which, I’ve been told, decisions are made simply by an inner voice. I fear not knowing myself enough.

‘You don’t know my mind, you don’t know my kind’ — well, Red Hot Chili Peppers, neither do I.

I think I know what I’m afraid of — becoming a cog in a wheel, losing my drive to succeed. And in a way, I can only fear something that I can prevent, otherwise the inevitability shouldn’t come as a shock. So the fear itself tells me that some part of me still believes this is mine to avoid. But whether it is a generic dread, the kind everyone carries, or something specific to me, I have no way of knowing until I test it. Proving myself is the only experiment that returns the answer. And proving myself would in turn require a firm belief of already having proved myself. Thus, I need to make decisions with the mindset that, in a later timeframe, I’ve already achieved them.

Now a question arises: what must I inculcate in myself to breed such a mindset? Is all that it calls for a big ego and a narcissistic, greater-than-thou attitude? But it has to be deeper than a need to prove myself to others. Something about the mountain I am climbing for myself, not for others to see that I’m climbing it. My values tell me, narcissism is probably not the answer. Then is it discipline and consistency? Just the ability to show up, regardless of result. commit. commit. And commit again. But what about burning out? Is passion somehow involved? What is passion? Are you meant for something, or do you choose your path? Are you but a deterministic output of all the rooms you’ve been in, the people you’ve met, the conversations you’ve had, the opinions and thoughts you’ve heard? OR IS THERE SOMETHING MORE.

And as we wind down the road, our shadows taller than our soul, there walks a lady we all know, who shines white light and wants to show, how everything still turns to gold. And if you listen very hard, the tune will come to you at last. To be a rock and not to roll.

And I don’t just say all this. Somewhere interspersed between the sophomoric musings and the lyrics, I believe I have been able to capture a part of the problem, which I don’t claim to be unique to me. In fact, far from it. Decision-making has always been a human problem. As for whether there is something more — I’m no longer sure that’s a question thinking can answer. Maybe the something more is not found by thinking at all, but in the doing. So maybe the solution, in some cases, is to just stop thinking and start doing. ‘Just do it’.
